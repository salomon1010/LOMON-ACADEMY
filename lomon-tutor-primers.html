NLB
Positive & Negative Connotation
Vocabulary · Grade 7 · Language Arts
Open IXL NLB ↗
Read for 5 min · then open IXL
The Rule
Connotation is the emotional feeling a word carries — not its dictionary definition. Two words can mean the same thing but feel completely different.

Positive connotation = the word feels admiring, warm, or respectful.
Negative connotation = the word feels critical, cold, or dismissive.
Neutral connotation = the word just states a fact with no emotional charge.
See It
All three words below mean "a person who saves money." Same denotation — completely different feeling:
Positive"She is very thrifty." — feels like a compliment: smart, careful, admirable.
Neutral"She is very economical." — just a fact, no judgment either way.
Negative"She is very cheap." — feels like a criticism: stingy, selfish, unwilling to give.
The Trick
On IXL, ask yourself: "If someone used this word about me, would I feel good or bad?"
If good → positive. If bad → negative. If neither → neutral.

Watch for context: the sentence around the word often gives you clues about which direction the author intended.
3 More Pairs
Positive → Negativeconfident → arrogant · curious → nosy · assertive → pushy
Quick self-check before IXL
Which word has a more negative connotation: "slender" or "scrawny"? And why? Say your answer out loud before you go. Then check on IXL.
QWF
Subordinating Conjunctions
Grammar · Grade 7 · Language Arts
Open IXL QWF ↗
Read for 5 min · then open IXL
The Rule
A subordinating conjunction connects a dependent clause to a main clause and shows the logical relationship between them.

The 5 you must know: because · although · since · while · unless

Comma rule: If the dependent clause comes FIRST → use a comma. If it comes second → no comma needed.
See It
because = reasonShe studied hard because she wanted to ace the test. (why she studied)
although = surpriseAlthough it rained, they played outside. (unexpected outcome — comma after clause)
unless = conditionI won't stop unless you give me a good reason. (only if something else happens)
The Trick
Ask: what is the relationship between the two ideas?
Reason → because / since  ·  Surprise/contrast → although  ·  Same time → while  ·  Condition → unless
Quick self-check before IXL
Fill in the blank: "___ she was nervous, she performed perfectly." Which conjunction fits? Say why. Then open IXL.

BIO
Classify Symbiotic Relationships 🔬 Science
Life Science · Grade 7 · Critical Thinking
Open IXL Science ↗
5 min primer · builds analytical thinking
The Rule
Symbiosis = a long-term relationship between two different species. There are 3 types:

Mutualism — both species benefit. (+/+)
Commensalism — one benefits, the other is unaffected. (+/0)
Parasitism — one benefits, the other is harmed. (+/−)
See It
Mutualism +/+Bees collect nectar (benefit: food) and pollinate flowers (benefit: reproduction). Both win.
Commensalism +/0Barnacles attach to whales to travel long distances. The whale is not helped or harmed.
Parasitism +/−A tapeworm lives inside a dog, taking its nutrients. Dog loses, tapeworm wins.
Quick self-check before IXL
A clownfish hides in a sea anemone. The anemone's stinging tentacles protect the clownfish. The clownfish chases away fish that eat the anemone. What type of symbiosis is this?
GF5
Complete the Opinion Passage with an Example
Writing Strategies · Grade 6 · Language Arts
Open IXL GF5 ↗
Read for 5 min · then open IXL
The Rule
An opinion passage has 3 parts: Opinion → Reason → Example.

The opinion is what you believe. The reason explains why. The example is a real, specific detail that proves your reason is true. Without an example, your reason floats — it's not convincing.
See It
OpinionI think libraries are the most important buildings in a community.
ReasonThey give everyone free access to knowledge and learning.
Example (this is what IXL tests)For instance, our school library has over 3,000 books that any student can borrow for free, including books about science, history, and every type of story imaginable.
The example is specific — it has a number, a place, a detail. "Libraries are great" is not an example. "Our library has 3,000 free books" is.
The Trick
When IXL asks you to complete the passage, look for the sentence that has a specific, real-sounding detail. Opinion sentences use "I think/believe." Reason sentences say "because/since." Example sentences use for instance, for example, such as, or a specific fact.
Quick self-check before IXL
Read this: "I think kids should eat breakfast every morning. Eating breakfast helps your brain focus. ___." Which sentence best completes it: A) "Breakfast is really important." or B) "For example, studies show students who eat breakfast score 20% higher on tests." Say why before you open IXL.
36M
Use Adverbs to Compare
Grammar · Grade 6 · Language Arts
Open IXL 36M ↗
Read for 5 min · then open IXL
The Rule
Comparative adverbs compare two actions. Superlative adverbs compare three or more.

Short adverbs: add -er (comparative) / -est (superlative) → fast → faster → fastest
Long adverbs: use more / most → carefully → more carefully → most carefully
Irregular: well → better → best  ·  badly → worse → worst
See It
Comparing 2 thingsKendra runs faster than her sister. (two people — use comparative)
Comparing 3+ thingsOf all the runners, she ran most quickly. (group of many — use superlative)
The Trick
Count the things being compared. Two things? → use -er or more. Three or more? → use -est or most. Never say "more faster" or "most fastest" — that's a double comparison error.
Quick self-check before IXL
Fill in the blank: "Of all my friends, Kendra sings ___." (beautiful) — should you use "more beautifully" or "most beautifully"? Say why.
QEB
Solve Two-Step Equations
Algebra · Math · Grade 6
Open IXL QEB ↗
Read for 5 min · then open IXL
The Rule
A two-step equation needs two operations to isolate the variable. Always work in this order:

Step 1: Undo addition or subtraction (using the opposite operation).
Step 2: Undo multiplication or division (using the opposite operation).

Whatever you do to one side, do the exact same thing to the other side.
See It
Solve: 2x + 3 = 11

Step 1 — undo the +3Subtract 3 from both sides → 2x = 8
Step 2 — undo the ×2Divide both sides by 2 → x = 4

Check: 2(4) + 3 = 11 ✓ Always check your answer by plugging it back in.
Quick self-check before IXL
Solve this: 3x − 5 = 16. Work it out step by step before you open IXL. What is x? (Answer: x = 7)

Life Science · Grade 6 · Critical Thinking
Open IXL Science ↗
5 min primer · builds vocabulary
The Rule
Cells are the basic unit of all living things. Key vocabulary:

Cell membrane — controls what goes in and out (like a security guard).
Nucleus — the control center, contains DNA (like the brain).
Cytoplasm — the jelly-like fluid that fills the cell.
Mitochondria — produces energy for the cell (the "powerhouse").
Cell wall — rigid outer layer found in plant cells only, not animal cells.

8ZJ
Choose the Correct Possessive Pronoun
Grammar · Grade 4 · Language Arts · Priority gap
Open IXL 8ZJ ↗
Read for 5 min · this is your #1 priority skill
The Rule
A possessive pronoun shows who owns something. There are two kinds:

Used before a noun: my · your · his · her · its · our · their
"That is my book."

Used alone (no noun after): mine · yours · his · hers · its · ours · theirs
"That book is mine."
See It
Correct"That is her pencil." · "The pencil is hers."
Incorrect"That is hers pencil." ✗ (never put mine/yours/hers before a noun)
The Trick
Look at what comes after the blank. If a noun comes after (pencil, book, dog) → use my/your/his/her/our/their. If nothing comes after → use mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs.
Quick self-check before IXL
Choose the right word: "Is that ___ lunchbox?" (your / yours) and "That lunchbox is ___." (your / yours) Say both answers before you open IXL.

The Rule
Add -s to most words → cat → cats, dog → dogs, book → books

Add -es when the word ends in: s · ss · sh · ch · x · z
→ bus → buses, dish → dishes, watch → watches, box → boxes

The Rule
A picture graph uses symbols or pictures to represent data. Always check the KEY first — it tells you how many each picture is worth.

PLT
Flowering Plant Life Cycles 🔬 Science
Life Science · Grade 4 · Critical Thinking
Open IXL Science ↗
5 min primer · builds vocabulary
The Rule
Flowering plants go through 4 stages in order:

1. Seed → 2. Germination (seed sprouts, roots grow) → 3. Seedling/Growth (stem, leaves appear) → 4. Adult plant with flowers → Pollination → Seeds form → cycle repeats.

Key word: pollination = pollen moves from one flower to another (by bees, wind, or water) which allows seeds to form.
Quick self-check before IXL
What stage comes right after germination? And what must happen for a plant to produce seeds? Say both answers before you open IXL.
U72
Combine Sentences — Predicates
Writing · Grade 3 · Language Arts · #1 Priority
Open IXL U72 ↗
Read for 5 min · do this skill FIRST every day
The Rule
When two sentences have the same subject (who/what), you can join their predicates (the action part) using and.

This makes your writing flow better and sound less choppy.
See It
Two choppy sentences"The dog ran across the yard. The dog barked at the squirrel."
Combined — much better!"The dog ran across the yard and barked at the squirrel."
The subject (the dog) appears only once. The two actions are joined with and.
The Trick
Step 1: Find the subject in both sentences — are they the same?
Step 2: If yes, keep the subject once, add both predicates, join with and.
Step 3: Read your new sentence out loud. Does it sound smooth?
Quick self-check before IXL
Combine these: "Angely jumped rope." and "Angely sang a song." What is the combined sentence? Say it out loud before you open IXL. (Answer: Angely jumped rope and sang a song.)
TGU
Use Context Clues to Find Word Meaning
Reading Strategies · Grade 3 · Language Arts
Open IXL TGU ↗
Read for 5 min · then open IXL
The Rule
When you see a word you don't know, don't skip it — look at the words and sentences around it. They give you context clues that help you figure out what it means.

The sentence is like a puzzle. The mystery word is one piece, and the other pieces help you figure out its shape.
See It
Example"The puppy was famished after running all day, so she gobbled up her entire bowl of food."

What does famished mean? The clues say: she ran all day → she ate her whole bowl fast. Famished must mean very hungry.
The Trick
Step 1: Find the mystery word. Step 2: Read the whole sentence. Step 3: Ask — "What is happening here?" Step 4: Guess the meaning. Step 5: Try your guess — does the sentence make sense?
Quick self-check before IXL
Read this: "She was so exhausted from playing that she fell asleep the moment her head touched the pillow." What does exhausted mean? Say your answer before opening IXL.

The Rule
Every number has 3 place values: hundreds · tens · ones

Reading a number: The digit on the left = hundreds, middle = tens, right = ones.
The number 347 = 3 hundreds + 4 tens + 7 ones = 300 + 40 + 7

Writing a number from parts: 5 hundreds + 2 tens + 8 ones = 528
See It
Number → Parts462 = 4 hundreds + 6 tens + 2 ones = 400 + 60 + 2
Parts → Number7 hundreds + 0 tens + 3 ones = 703 (don't forget the zero!)
Quick self-check before IXL
What number is: 8 hundreds + 1 ten + 5 ones? Say the answer before you open IXL. (Answer: 815)
ANI
Animal Life Cycle Diagrams 🔬 Science
Life Science · Grade 3 · Critical Thinking
Open IXL Science ↗
5 min primer · builds world knowledge
The Rule
Animals go through life cycle stages — birth, growth, reproduction, death. Most animals follow one of two patterns:

Simple life cycle: Egg → Young → Adult → Egg again (dogs, birds, fish)
Metamorphosis: Egg → Larva → Pupa → Adult (butterflies, frogs)

Key vocabulary: larva = baby form (caterpillar) · pupa = resting stage (cocoon) · metamorphosis = big body change
Quick self-check before IXL
A butterfly starts as an egg. What are the 3 stages that come after the egg before it becomes an adult butterfly? Say all 3 in order before you open IXL.
