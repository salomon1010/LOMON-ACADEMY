The principle that runs everything
Consistency, not intensity. Systems, not intentions.

No Escalation · No Emotion · Just Execution

Daily Script

You don't react — you execute. This is the difference between chaos and control. Read these scripts before tough moments. Practice them out loud once. Then use them word-for-word.

A. Giving an instruction
Step 1 — Say it once. Clear. Direct.
"Kendra, start your homework now."
Then stop talking. No follow-up. Wait 10 seconds.
Step 2 — If no action in ~10 seconds
"This is your second notice. If you don't start now, you lose screen time tonight."
No discussion. State it once.
Step 3 — If still no action
"You chose to lose screen time today."
Enforce immediately. No arguing. No reversing.
B. When child argues / negotiates
Child says: "But I don't want to..."
"This is not a discussion. Do it now or accept the consequence."
Repeat once only.
If they continue arguing
"You're choosing the consequence."
End interaction. Walk away.
C. When child refuses homework
Step 1 — Set the expectation
"You try first for 10 minutes. Then I help."
Step 2 — If they refuse
"No homework = no screen time. Your choice."
⁠Do not help until they start. This protects independence.
D. Disrespect (tone / attitude)
Immediate interrupt
"Stop. That's disrespectful. Try again correctly."
If repeated
"You've lost privileges for today."
No lecture. No emotional reaction.
E. When they do something right
Most parents miss this — then wonder why behavior doesn't stick
"I saw you start your homework without being told. Good."
Short. Direct. No exaggeration. Acknowledge the specific action, not a generic compliment.
F. When YOU lose control (you will)
Reset fast — model self-correction
"I handled that wrong. Let's restart."
Then reapply the rule calmly. No ego. Just correction. Your kids learn how to self-correct from watching you do it.
Your personal rules — if you break these, nothing works
❌ Don't repeat instructions more than twice
❌ Don't threaten consequences you won't enforce
❌ Don't ignore bad behavior "just today"
❌ Don't let your mood control your response
4-Level Enforcement Ladder · Critical Rule: Consequences Happen Immediately, Not Later

Enforcement Ladder

When a rule is broken, you don't think — you escalate one level on this ladder. Predictable. Fair. Not emotional. The kids know exactly what happens.

1
Minor Issue → Reminder + Correction
First time something happens that day. Not done before. Small slip.
Action: Quick reminder — "Hey, remember the rule." That's it.
2
Repeated Behavior → Loss of Screen Time (Same Day)
They've already been reminded once today. Now they choose the consequence.
Action: All screens off for the rest of today. No exceptions.
3
Serious or Repeated → Loss of Privileges (24–48 hours)
Lying. Disrespect after warning. Refusing to do homework. Behavior that affects the family.
Action: 24–48 hours no privileges (screens, friends, dessert, free time). Apply immediately.
4
Pattern Behavior → Weekly Restriction + Family Meeting Discussion
Same problem multiple times in one week. Pattern of disrespect. System being challenged.
Action: Full weekly restriction + named at Sunday family meeting. The whole family hears the issue and the decision.
⚠ Critical rule — never break this
Consequences happen immediately, not later.
If you say "we'll deal with it tonight" — you've already lost. The brain only connects behavior to consequence when they're tied together in time. "Tomorrow you lose..." doesn't work for kids. This minute. This hour. Today.

Build Independence Gradually · Not Suddenly

Independence Framework

The goal is responsibility without supervision — but only at the right pace. Each kid has different capacity. Push too fast and they fail. Hold them too tight and they never grow.

The Independence Rule
Step 1: Child tries alone first.
Step 2: Child asks for help if stuck.
Step 3: Parent supports — does not do.

If child skips Step 1 → no help is given. They go back and try first. This is non-negotiable.
Trust building progression
If child succeeds at a responsibility → increase it the next week (more independence, more trust, more privilege).

If child fails → correct, don't immediately remove the responsibility. Removing too fast teaches "if I fail enough, I get out of it." That's the wrong lesson.

Pattern of failure → step them back ONE level on the framework. Rebuild from there.

Weekly Family Meeting — Sunday 30 minutes

Weekly Check-in

The single most powerful habit you can build. Every person speaks, every person is heard. Problems surface early. Wins are celebrated. The week ahead gets planned together.

Step 1 · 5 min · Everyone
🌟 What was your best moment this week? Start with the youngest, go around the circle. No interruptions.
Step 2 · 5 min · Everyone
😤 Was there anything hard or unfair this week? Each person gets 60 seconds. No defending — just listening.
Step 3 · 5 min · Kids
🗳️ Family vote topic this week: Use the Vote tab to let kids pick something for the week ahead.
Step 4 · 5 min · Parents only (after kids)
💍 Salomon & Pascaline: What do we need from each other next week? One sentence each — specific and kind.
Step 5 · 5 min · Everyone
📅 What are we looking forward to next week? Each person names one thing. End on a high note.
